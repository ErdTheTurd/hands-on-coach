Ghost hands show you where your fingers go.

Fingerwiz is a free app for iPhone, iPad, and Apple Vision Pro. It teaches piano, guitar chords, knots, and ASL fingerspelling by putting a glowing, translucent hand on the thing you are practicing. You match the glow.

Made by Edward Dunn. The app runs on your device: no account, no ads, no tracking.

Piano

Finger numbers first, then songs.

A lesson starts with a highlighted key and a ghost finger already on it — thumb on C4, for example. You place your finger to match. Early lessons cover finger numbers and hand position. Later ones move into left-hand basics, scales, chords, and songs.

Free Play stays with you and hints at the fingering as you explore. You can use a real keyboard, a paper keyboard, or the one built into the app.

Guitar, knots, and sign work the same way.

Guitar is six beginner chord lessons. Ghost fingers mark the frets on a virtual fretboard. The microphone checks that the chord is actually ringing — not just that you tapped the right spots.

Knots are five essential knots, broken into steps. Each step has an illustration and grip poses, so you can see where the left and right hands go before you pull the rope tight.

ASL fingerspelling covers the alphabet, A to Z, across seven lessons. A ghost hand holds the letter. You copy it. The camera checks your handshape on the device — nothing is recorded or uploaded — and tells you when it matches.

You can try every lesson without an instrument.

Demo Mode uses virtual instruments, so you do not need a piano, a guitar, or a camera free. It is on the first time you open the app.

There are also daily goals, streaks, and ten achievements, plus optional reminders. All of that stays on the device. Progress is not uploaded anywhere. In Free Play, ghost hands keep hinting at fingering while you wander around the keys — no score, just practice.

Apple Vision Pro

A floating 3D piano in the room.

On Vision Pro, piano practice can sit in an immersive space. The keyboard floats in front of you. The same ghost-hand coaching still applies, and hand tracking stays on the device.
